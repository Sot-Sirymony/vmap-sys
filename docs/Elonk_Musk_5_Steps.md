Yes, you probably mean Musk's "algorithm," his 5-step method for engineering and manufacturing. He has described it on Starbase tours and in interviews, and Walter Isaacson's 2023 biography covers it. The steps go in this order:

1. **Make the requirements less dumb.** Question every requirement, even ones from smart people, and especially ones from smart people, because those are the ones nobody questions. Each requirement should have a named person responsible for it, not just "a department."

2. **Delete the part or process.** Remove whatever you can. His rule of thumb: if you don't end up adding back at least 10% of what you deleted, you didn't delete enough.

3. **Simplify and optimize.** Only do this after steps 1 and 2. He says the most common mistake smart engineers make is optimizing something that shouldn't exist at all.

4. **Accelerate cycle time.** Speed up the process, but only once the first three steps are done. Speeding up a bad process just gets you to the wrong result faster.

5. **Automate.** This comes last. Musk admits he made this mistake on the Tesla Model 3 production line: he automated too early, then had to tear out robots that were automating steps that should have been deleted.

The order is the main point. People tend to start at step 5 or 3, but going backwards wastes effort on things that should never have existed. It's a common framework outside engineering too, for business processes, software, and even personal workflows.
